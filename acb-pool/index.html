What the calculator assumes

  • One pool per coin. All units of the same coin are identical property, wherever they are held, so they share one average cost (ITA s.47, IT-387R2). An exchange's own tax report can only see its own trades.
  • Moving coins between your own accounts changes nothing. A transfer is not a sale and does not change the ACB. Network fees paid in the coin itself are a small disposition the calculator does not model; enter them as a sale if they matter.
  • Fees. A purchase fee is added to the cost. A selling fee is an outlay that reduces the gain.
  • Amounts are in Canadian dollars on the day of each trade. Crypto-to-crypto trades are a sale of one coin and a purchase of the other at fair market value; enter each coin in its own calculator.
  • Superficial losses. A loss is tested against every purchase from 30 days before to 30 days after the sale, on any exchange. The denied part is added to the pool's ACB once the 30 days have passed, so later sales use the higher cost. Purchases by a spouse or a corporation you control also count but are not modelled.
  • Capital, not business income. If you trade as a business, gains are fully taxable income and the superficial-loss rule does not apply.
  • Half of a net capital gain is taxable (ITA s.38). A net capital loss can be carried back three years or forward indefinitely against taxable capital gains (ITA s.111).
  • Same engine as TaxLedger. The arithmetic matches TaxLedger's engine to the cent on 512 test runs, 4,110 trades and 264 superficial-loss events.
